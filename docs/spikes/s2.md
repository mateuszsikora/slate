# S-2 — Render performance

**Verdict: PASS at twelve tiles, and §6.2 is right about the bounce buffer.** A saturated
design.md §3.2 page — twelve tiles, four of them animated, ten entity updates per second, radio
active — renders in **13 789 us at p50 and 17 443 us at p95** against a **26 441 us** frame
budget, and **none of 1 817 frames** exceeded it. The maintainer watched the panel and confirmed
no visible stutter, tearing or flicker, which is what §13 actually asks for.

The two things this spike was to determine:

- **Maximum tile count per page: the grid is the limit, and it is close to being the right
  one.** Twelve tiles use 66 % of the frame budget at p95. Twenty tiles on a relaxed 5 × 4 grid
  still never miss a frame but reach **98 % of budget at the worst frame** — a wider grid is
  not on the table.
- **One framebuffer or two: two, VSYNC-gated, with the bounce buffer kept.** Tearing with a
  single framebuffer is measurable and invisible; the second framebuffer earns its place by
  *returning* 77 832 B of internal SRAM. Removing the bounce buffer looks free in every counter
  and destroys the picture — see [The bounce buffer](#the-bounce-buffer-6-2-is-right).

Two corrections to design.md fall out of the measurement: **§6.2's draw buffer does not fit
once WiFi is up**, and **§13's "20 tiles" is not expressible in §3.2's grid**.

**Two of this document's own numbers were wrong before they were right**, and both errors are
described rather than quietly fixed: the scene ran with one animated tile instead of four for
most of the spike, understating frame work by 55 %; and the bounce buffer was initially
reported as unnecessary on the strength of counters that could not see the failure it prevents.
See [Method notes](#method-notes).

Measured 2026-08-07 against issue #2.

**This spike had to bring the panel up first.** S-1 ran on a dummy display and S-3 linked the

### Pin map and timings, verified by running them

Cross-checked between the Waveshare wiki's pinout table for this board and an independent
ESPHome package for the same board (`inytar/waveshare-esp32-s3-touch-lcd-7-esphome`). They
agree pin for pin. `waveshareteam/ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-7B` is a **different variant** and was
deliberately not used as a source — §6.3 records what this board's documentation is worth.

| Signal | GPIO | | Signal | GPIO |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HSYNC | 46 | | B3…B7 | 14, 38, 18, 17, 10 |
| VSYNC | 3  | | G2…G7 | 39, 0, 45, 48, 47, 21 |
| DE    | 5  | | R3…R7 | 1, 2, 42, 41, 40 |
| PCLK  | 7  | | I²C (SDA/SCL) | 8 / 9 |

The panel has no reset line of its own on the SoC: **CH422G EXIO3 resets the panel, EXIO2 is
the backlight enable, EXIO1 resets the touch controller.** A correct pin map with the expander
untouched produces a black screen, which is indistinguishable from a wrong pin map.

```
h_total = 800 + 8 (back) + 8 (front) + 4 (pulse) = 820 pclk
v_total = 480 + 16 + 16 + 4                      = 516 lines
16 MHz / (820 × 516) = 37.81 Hz   →  26 441 us per frame
```

Measured VSYNC period: **26 441 us, 37.82 Hz**. The arithmetic and the panel agree, which is
what licenses every "% of budget" below.

## Tile count

The issue asks for 20 tiles. §3.2 fixes the content area at a 4 × 3 grid and the smallest tile
at 1×1, so **a page cannot hold more than twelve**. Reporting a maximum of 20 would name a
number no configuration can express. Both were measured: twelve as the saturated page the
verdict is about, twenty as an overload on a relaxed 5 × 4 grid, to answer whether a wider grid
is even on the table.

Recommended configuration, radio active, four animated tiles, budget 26 441 us:

| | 12 tiles (§3.2 saturated) | 20 tiles (5 × 4 overload) |
|---|---:|---:|
| frame work p50 | 13 789 us — **52 %** | 17 130 us — 65 % |
| frame work p95 | 17 443 us — **66 %** | 21 839 us — 83 % |
| frame work max | 19 796 us — 75 % | 25 938 us — **98 %** |
| frames over budget | **0 / 1 817** | 0 / 1 814 |
| effective frame rate | 35.6 fps | 34.7 fps |
| page rebuild | 186 ms | 277 ms |

Twelve tiles leave a third of the budget spare at p95 and never miss a frame. Twenty tiles also
never miss one, but the worst frame lands at **98 % of the budget** — no margin at all for the
components §15 defers, on a page size no configuration can currently express anyway. **The limit
on tiles per page is the grid, there is no reason to change §3.2, and widening it to 5 × 4 would
be spending the last of the frame budget.**

Percentiles, not averages, throughout: stutter is a tail phenomenon and a mean frame time hides
exactly the frames that are visible.

## Framebuffers — the §6.2 question

Three configurations, all with the radio active and twelve tiles. The last column is the
recommendation.

These three runs predate the animation fix described in [Method notes](#method-notes), so their
absolute frame times are measured against a lighter scene than §13 specifies and are not
comparable with the table above. They are kept because all three share the same scene, which is
what makes the comparison between them valid — and because the two decisions they settle
(tearing, and where the memory goes) do not depend on how much of the screen moves.

| | 1 FB | 2 FB, no VSYNC gate | **2 FB + VSYNC gate** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| frame work p50 | 5 521 us | 5 561 us | 9 011 us |
| frame work p95 | 10 168 us | 10 064 us | 12 599 us |
| torn frames / s (measured) | 11.2 | 0.0 | **0.0** |
| tearing (maintainer's eye) | not visible | — | **not visible** |
| flicker (maintainer's eye) | not reported | **visible on text and icons** | **none** |
| internal SRAM free | 111 651 B | 189 483 B | **189 279 B** |
| PSRAM free | 5 421 996 B | 4 653 992 B | 4 621 224 B |
| verdict | works, but see below | rejected | **recommended** |

Three things in that table are worth more than the numbers.

### Tearing is real, measurable, and invisible

With one framebuffer LVGL renders into the buffer the panel is scanning out. The harness
timestamps every flush and compares it against the scan-out position derived from the VSYNC
interrupt, so a flush that writes rows the beam is currently passing is counted. At 12 tiles
that is **5.8 torn frames per second at LVGL's default refresh and 11.2 with the radio active**
— and asked to look for a horizontal seam, the maintainer reported seeing nothing, twice.

So the tearing that §6.2 worries about exists at a rate of roughly one frame in three and is
below the threshold of visibility on this content. **It is not the reason to spend 750 KB.**

### The recommended configuration looks like the slowest one, and is not

Frame work at p50 rises from 5.5 ms to 9.0 ms when VSYNC gating is switched on. That is not a
cost: gating makes LVGL render once per scan-out instead of twice, so each frame carries twice
as much accumulated change. The panel cannot display more than 37.8 frames a second whatever
LVGL does, and the extra frames were being thrown away — see the flicker below.

Reading the raw numbers here needs care, and getting it wrong the first time nearly put the
recommendation in the bin. LVGL's render window contains the flush, the flush contains the
VSYNC wait, and the wait is idle time: in the gated runs it is **15 373 us of a 24 640 us
window**. Reported naively as "render + flush", the recommended configuration measures 40 ms a
frame and looks like a failure. The reports subtract the wait and say so.

### The second framebuffer pays for itself in the scarce memory

The interesting column is internal SRAM, not PSRAM. With one framebuffer LVGL needs two draw
buffers in internal DMA-capable SRAM; with two framebuffers in direct mode it renders straight
into PSRAM and allocates none. Measured with the radio up, that is **77 832 B of internal SRAM
returned in exchange for 768 KB of PSRAM** — and PSRAM still has 4.6 MB free while internal
SRAM is what S-3 nearly ran out of at 88 % DIRAM occupancy, before M1's WiFi stack, HTTP
server, WebSocket client and state store are written.

## Two things design.md gets wrong

### §6.2's draw buffer does not fit once WiFi is up

§6.2 specifies "LVGL draw buffer: ~1/10 screen, internal SRAM". LVGL wants two so rendering and
flushing overlap, which is 2 × 76 800 = 153 600 B. With the radio started there is not that
much internal DMA-capable memory left:

```
E (1076) s2.board: draw buffers (2 × 76800 B) do not fit: 104167 B internal free
S2FAIL,draw_buf_bytes,76800,int_dma_free,104167
```

The first version of this harness asserted here and boot-looped. **A single-framebuffer M1
following §6.2 literally would not boot with WiFi enabled**, and the failure would arrive as a
reset loop weeks after the section was written. The single-framebuffer runs above therefore use
24-line buffers (2 × 38 400 B) and report which size they used; the recommended
double-buffered configuration sidesteps the question entirely.

### §13's "20 tiles" is not expressible

Covered above. §13 should either say twelve, or say that twenty is a deliberate overload
target. The written answer to "maximum tile count per page" is *the grid*.

## The LVGL refresh period, which nobody asked about

The most useful finding of this spike was not in its brief.

At LVGL's default `CONFIG_LV_DEF_REFR_PERIOD` of 33 ms the maintainer reported visible judder
on scrolling text. The numbers had been saying so all along and were being read past: the
frame interval sat at exactly **33 001 us**. That is a constant, not a load.

33 ms is 30.3 Hz. The panel scans at 37.8 Hz. Neither is a multiple of the other, so each
rendered frame is held for one or two scan-outs in an irregular pattern — invisible on a
changing digit, obvious on anything moving at a constant rate. A §7.5 marquee label is the most
sensitive object on the screen and is exactly where it showed.

Dropping the period to 10 ms, same scene, 20 tiles, single framebuffer:

| | 33 ms (default) | 10 ms |
|---|---:|---:|
| effective frame rate | 27.9 fps | **76.8 fps** |
| frame interval p50 | 33 001 us | 10 999 us |
| frame work p50 | 9 573 us | **6 804 us** |
| pixels per frame p50 | 6 380 | 4 988 |

Rendering got **cheaper** while running nearly three times as often, because each frame carries
less accumulated change. Smoothness here did not cost performance; it released it.

**M1 should not ship the default.** With the recommended configuration the refresh period wants
to be short enough that VSYNC, not LVGL's timer, sets the pace.

## Flicker: rendering faster than the panel

Setting the refresh period to 10 ms with two framebuffers produced a new symptom the maintainer
spotted immediately — a flicker on text and icons, present with page switching disabled, so not
a rebuild artifact.

The cause is in the ESP-IDF RGB driver. With a bounce buffer configured, a framebuffer switch
requested by `esp_lcd_panel_draw_bitmap` does not take effect when it is called. `cur_fb_index`
is set at once, but the bounce filler keeps reading `bb_fb_index` until the scan wraps at a
frame boundary (`esp_lcd_panel_rgb.c`):

```c
if (panel->bounce_pos_px >= panel->fb_size / bytes_per_pixel) {
    panel->bounce_pos_px = 0;
    panel->bb_fb_index = panel->cur_fb_index;   // the switch lands here
```

So the panel adopts whichever buffer happens to be current at a frame boundary. LVGL running at
67 fps against a 37.8 Hz panel submits about two buffers per displayed frame; the skipped one
is a frame of motion that never reaches the glass, and moving content alternates between two
versions of itself. It is absent at the 33 ms default for an unsatisfying reason — 30.3 Hz is
*slower* than the panel, so nothing is ever skipped.

Waiting for the VSYNC that follows the switch fixes it: frame interval **26 221 us** against a
panel period of 26 441 us, one rendered frame per scan-out. Confirmed on the panel.

**A wrong fix was measured first and is worth recording.** The obvious explanation — that the
two framebuffers were out of sync because LVGL only renders invalidated areas — is wrong: LVGL
9.5 already does that, in `refr_sync_areas()` in `lv_refr.c`. Copying the areas again by hand
moved frame work from 5.6 ms to 16.1 ms and fixed nothing, because the flicker was never a sync
problem. The measurement caught it; reasoning about it did not.

## Radio, without credentials

The maintainer declined to hand over WiFi credentials, which turns out not to matter: §6.2's
artifact comes from the radio contending for the memory bus the panel's DMA streams from and
from the WiFi task's interrupt load, and both are present the moment the PHY is on. The runs
marked "radio" run AP-mode beaconing, a continuous active scan, and promiscuous receive, with
no association to any network. The promiscuous callback reads `sig_len` and nothing else; no
payload is inspected, stored or transmitted.

It is a real load rather than an asserted one: **1 649 to 2 573 frames in the air per 51-second
run**, counted. Cost of the radio, matched pair at 12 tiles, one framebuffer:

| | radio idle | radio active |
|---|---:|---:|
| frame work p50 | 5 186 us | 5 521 us (+6.5 %) |
| effective frame rate | 69.5 fps | 67.3 fps |
| internal SRAM free | 223 823 B | 111 651 B |

Six per cent of frame time and **112 KB of internal SRAM** — the memory is the part that
matters, and it is what breaks §6.2 above.

## The bounce buffer — §6.2 is right

This is the finding that reversed itself, and the way it reversed is the point.

§6.2 states the bounce buffer is required because WiFi activity otherwise causes visible
artifacts. Every counter in this harness says the opposite:

| | with bounce | without bounce |
|---|---:|---:|
| frame work p50 | 8 915 us | **8 568 us** |
| frame work max | 15 256 us | **14 818 us** |
| torn frames / s | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| internal SRAM free | 189 283 B | **217 003 B** |

Without it the panel is 4 % faster per frame and **27 720 B** cheaper in internal SRAM, because
the DMA reads PSRAM directly and the CPU stops refilling an intermediate buffer. On that
evidence the first version of this document recommended keeping the buffer only as "the
conservative choice" and handed the question to #6 as unresolved.

**It was then put to a blind test and failed immediately.** Two builds differing in nothing but
the bounce buffer, both with page switching disabled so that the confound from an earlier
observation was gone, both labelled only `A` and `B` on the panel so the observer could not know
which was which. Reports, verbatim:

- **B** (bounce buffer present): "the scrolling text has slight fps drops... but I have to stare
  at one point to see it".
- **A** (no bounce buffer): "this is rubbish — the page slides and lags, and at the top I can see
  what should be at the bottom".

Bottom-of-screen content appearing at the top of a picture that slides vertically is the
signature of **DMA starvation**: with no bounce buffer the RGB controller streams the
framebuffer straight out of PSRAM, and when it loses the race — against the radio and against
the CPU rendering the next frame into the same memory — the line pointer slips and the image
rolls. It is §6.2's artifact, appearing as a rolling picture rather than as a static tear.

**Keep the bounce buffer.** The 27 720 B it costs is not a trade-off against speed; it is the
price of the picture being correct at all.

Why the instrument could not see it: every counter here measures the *production* of frames —
render time, flush time, invalidated pixels, and a tear test derived from the VSYNC clock. The
failure happens after that, while the finished framebuffer is being clocked onto the glass.
Nothing on the CPU side is late; the frame work genuinely does get cheaper. **A measurement that
only watches the producer will call a broken display fast.**

## Page switching

The §6.4 rebuild path, twelve tiles, recommended configuration:

| | p50 | max |
|---|---:|---:|
| destroy tree | 1 576 us | 1 789 us |
| build tree | 39 348 us | 45 603 us |
| first frame on the panel | 140 209 us | 155 751 us |
| **total** | **187 ms** | 209 ms |

At 20 tiles this is 277 ms. §13's criterion is "no visible stutter when switching pages", and
the maintainer's verdict was that the switch is *abrupt* — the screen blanks and repaints —
but not stuttering. **That is a missing transition, not a performance failure**, and it belongs
to #20 rather than here.

## Rendering gaps, and a hole in the instrument

Asked to look at the recommended build, the maintainer reported "slight fps drops, but I have to
stare at one point to see it". The harness had been reporting a clean bill of health, because it
was measuring the wrong axis: percentiles of *frame time* cannot show a frame that was never
rendered.

Adding the interval between frames to the report made it visible — and made the animation defect
below visible with it:

| | gaps > 100 ms | rate | share of wall time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| one animated tile (as first measured) | 63 | 1.24 / s | **24 %** |
| four animated tiles (§13) | 15 | 0.29 / s | 7 % |

The gaps are not stalls. LVGL renders when something is invalidated, so a screen where nothing
moves correctly draws nothing — the 24 % figure was the harness under-loading itself, not the
runtime hesitating. The remaining 7 % is the §7.5 marquee pausing at the end of each scroll
cycle, which is by design.

Two things follow. **The metric belongs in any future measurement**: `s2_report.py` now prints
frame intervals alongside frame times, because the two answer different questions and only one
of them corresponds to what a person sees. And **"no visible stutter" is a claim about a screen
with four things moving on it** — a real dashboard where nothing changes for a minute will not
render for a minute, which is correct and will look like nothing at all.

## What this does not prove

- **No touch.** GT911 is not driven; the expander is used only for reset and backlight. Touch
  is #7, and an input device adds LVGL work this spike does not measure.
- **No Home Assistant.** The 10 updates/s are synthesised locally. The real path (§5.2 diffs
  over a WebSocket, JSON parsing, state store) adds CPU and heap traffic on the same core.
- **No sustained network throughput.** Beacons and scan responses are not a §11.1 OTA pushing
  1.5 MB over the same radio, which is a heavier bus load and remains unmeasured.
- **One panel, one temperature, one hour.** Nothing here speaks to thermal behaviour or to
  panel-to-panel variation.
- **The visual verdict is one person's eyes**, not instrumented. That is what §13 asks for, and
  it is also the strongest and the weakest thing here: the bounce-buffer decision was settled by
  an eye after every counter had got it wrong, and there is no counter in this harness that
  would have caught it. `lv_snapshot_take()` over the wire (§15's remote preview) would make
  this reproducible instead of anecdotal.

## Findings for other issues

- **#6 (display bring-up) inherits the whole bring-up.** Pin map and timings above, verified by
  running them. Recommended configuration: **two framebuffers, direct render mode, VSYNC-gated
  flush, bounce buffer of 10 lines**, LVGL refresh period well under the panel period. Do not
  size draw buffers per §6.2 — measure what is left after WiFi starts. **Keep the bounce
  buffer**: without it the picture rolls under radio load, and no CPU-side counter shows it.
- **#6 / §6.2 need amending** on two points: the draw buffer that does not fit, and the
  framebuffer count, whose justification is internal SRAM rather than tearing.
- **#20 (UI runtime) owns the page transition.** 187 ms of blank screen on every `PUT /config`
  is the "abrupt" the maintainer saw. Building the new tree off-screen and swapping would hide
  it; the build itself is only 39 ms of the 187.
- **#20 also inherits a tile-count answer:** twelve tiles at 66 % of budget at p95, with four
  tiles animating. There is room for components to grow, but a third of the budget, not the
  half this document claimed before the animation defect was found.
- **#28 (brightness) and anything touching the backlight**: it is CH422G EXIO2, binary, as §16
  anticipated.
- **§13's "20 tiles" should be corrected** to match §3.2, the same way §13's "two fonts" was
  corrected by S-3.

## Method notes

Three things about this measurement could have produced a confident wrong answer, and two of
them did before they were caught.

**The scene ran with one animated tile instead of four for most of this spike.** §13 asks for
"20 tiles including 4 animated". The harness selected them by index — every fifth tile — without
accounting for the fact that only light and cover have an animated variant; sensor and scene
ignore the flag. On a twelve-tile page those indices landed on one light and three tiles that
cannot animate. Every frame-time number was therefore measured against a lighter load than the
brief, understating frame work at p50 by **55 %** (8 915 us against 13 789 us). It was caught
by the maintainer reporting visible frame drops that the report said were not there, which led
to plotting frame *intervals* and finding a quarter of the run with nothing being drawn. The
tables in this document are the corrected runs; the framebuffer comparison is explicitly marked
as the lighter scene.

**Blind the observer when the observer knows the hypothesis.** The bounce-buffer question came
down to one subjective judgement, and by the time it was asked the maintainer had read three
drafts of the recommendation. The two builds were relabelled `A` and `B` on the panel, with the
mapping withheld until both had been described. The result contradicted the recommendation,
which is the outcome blinding exists to make possible.

**A printf inside a frame measures the UART.** Frame samples are accumulated in PSRAM and
printed after the run, never inside the measured window.

**A build tag that lies is worse than no build tag.** Five separate visual judgements were
needed from the maintainer, and twice an answer nearly landed against a build that had been
reflashed in between. A build tag rendered on the panel fixed that — and then mislabelled a
gated build as `nogate`, because `s2_scene.c` did not include the header where `S2_VSYNC_GATE`
is defined and the preprocessor treated it as zero. The maintainer caught it by reading a label
that contradicted the run. An instrument that identifies the experiment has to be verified like
any other part of it.

**An instrument must not change what it measures.** The first version of the tag was a fourth
child of the system bar and moved the page title out of place. It now hangs off the root.

**`SDKCONFIG_DEFAULTS` is consulted only when no `sdkconfig` exists.** This is the trap that
produced two false variants in S-3. Every run here builds with an explicit `-DSDKCONFIG` inside
its own build directory, and the resulting `sdkconfig.h` is grepped for the setting the variant
was supposed to change. The 10 ms refresh variant silently rebuilt the 33 ms configuration on
the first attempt and was caught this way.

## Reproducing

**The harness is deliberately not in the tree**, following S-1 and S-3. It is an instrument: a
standalone ESP-IDF app that brings the panel up, renders a stand-in dashboard and measures it.
A stale copy of a UI runtime sitting in `spikes/` gets read as reference code, and #6 and #20
should write the real thing deliberately.

It is preserved as the first commit of **PR #51** — the *parent* of the branch tip, since the
tip is the commit that removed it:

```bash
git fetch origin refs/pull/51/head:s2-harness
git checkout s2-harness~1 -- spikes/s2-render-performance
```

What that restores: `main/s2_board.c` (pin map, RGB panel, LVGL port, VSYNC and tear
instrumentation), `main/s2_scene.c` (the §7 components with real fonts), `main/s2_main.c` (the
run plan and the radio load), `tools/s2_report.py`, `tools/s2_compare.py` and `tools/s2_run.sh`.

```bash
tools/fonts/generate.sh                       # the real §8 faces, required
cd spikes/s2-render-performance
tools/s2_run.sh final -DS2_NUM_FBS=2 -DS2_BOUNCE=1 -DS2_TILES=12 -DS2_RADIO=1
python3 tools/s2_report.py /tmp/s2_final.log
```

Every run is one build. The knobs are `S2_NUM_FBS`, `S2_BOUNCE`, `S2_TILES`, `S2_DRAW_LINES`,
`S2_RADIO`, `S2_VSYNC_GATE` and `S2_VISUAL_SWITCH`; the LVGL refresh period is the
`sdkconfig.refr10` overlay. Each run ends in a visual phase that repeats the load indefinitely,
which is what the maintainer watched — the numbers are half the answer and §13's criterion is
the other half.
